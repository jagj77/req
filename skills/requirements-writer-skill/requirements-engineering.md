# Requirements Engineering - INCOSE Methodology

## Definition
Requirements Engineering (RE) is a systematic engineering discipline that encompasses all activities concerned with discovering, documenting, analyzing, validating, and managing the needs and constraints of stakeholders, and their transformation into formal, verifiable requirements.

## Core Principles (INCOSE SE Handbook)

### P1: Stakeholder-Centric Approach
- Identify all stakeholders early
- Understand their needs, constraints, and expectations
- Ensure diverse stakeholder perspectives are captured
- Maintain traceability from stakeholder needs to requirements

### P2: Structured Process
Requirements engineering shall follow a disciplined, repeatable process:
1. **Elicitation**: Gather needs from stakeholders
2. **Analysis**: Understand and structure needs
3. **Specification**: Document as formal requirements
4. **Verification**: Ensure requirements meet characteristics
5. **Validation**: Ensure requirements satisfy stakeholder intent
6. **Management**: Control changes and maintain traceability

### P3: Quality-Driven Requirements
Every requirement must be evaluated for:
- **Necessity**: Essential to system capability
- **Appropriateness**: Belongs at correct architecture level
- **Clarity**: Unambiguous, complete, singular
- **Feasibility**: Achievable within constraints
- **Verifiability**: Testable through inspection, analysis, demonstration, or test

### P4: Formal Specification
Requirements shall be expressed in a formal manner:
- Use SHALL for obligations (normative)
- Use structured language patterns
- Employ defined terminology from glossary
- Comply with grammatical and syntactic rules
- Make measurable where possible (quantify with units)

### P5: Traceability
Maintain complete traceability:
- Source (stakeholder need) → Requirement
- Requirement → Verification method
- Requirement → Design implementation
- Requirement → Test cases
- Enable impact analysis for change management

### P6: Configuration Management
- Establish baseline requirements
- Control all changes formally
- Document rationale for requirements and changes
- Version control throughout lifecycle
- Maintain requirements repository

### P7: Continuous Validation
- Review requirements against stakeholder needs
- Validate feasibility and resource constraints
- Confirm consistency and non-contradiction
- Verify completeness of requirement set
- Assess risk and mitigation strategies

## Connection to Lifecycle

**Conceptual Phase**: Stakeholder needs identified
↓
**Needs Analysis**: Needs documented and analyzed
↓
**Requirements Definition**: Formal requirements specified
↓
**Verification Planning**: Verification methods selected
↓
**Validation Planning**: Validation strategy defined
